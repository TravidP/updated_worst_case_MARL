\paragraph{Supplementary sparse-demand results.}
The isolated Grid campaign completed all 200 formal rollouts, plus four separate full-horizon controller smoke checks. All positive-demand OD routes passed passenger-compatible path and SUMO loading checks; the formal results passed complete-matrix, per-second sample, paired-seed, demand-hash, network-signature, and final-checkpoint integrity checks. No learning or WCE updates were performed during these evaluations.
As shown in \cref{tab:external_grid_sparse}, Online WCE does not improve every architecture on this profile. Its mean per-rollout queue reductions relative to Baseline are $-11.08\%$ for IA2C, $0.69\%$ for MA2C, $39.32\%$ for IQL-LR, and $50.57\%$ for PPO. MA2C has no significant paired difference after Holm correction ($p=1.00$); the IA2C deterioration and IQL-LR/PPO reductions have adjusted two-sided exact sign-flip $p=0.0391$ when correcting the 20 prespecified comparisons. The test assumes symmetry of paired differences under the null. Fixed WCE outperforms Online WCE on IQL-LR ($30.59$ versus $108.14$ queued vehicles).
Throughput and removal diagnostics constrain this interpretation. IQL-LR Online WCE still averages $802.8$ pending vehicles and $22.4$ teleports, compared with $5.4$ pending vehicles and $4.4$ teleports for Fixed WCE. MA2C Online WCE completes fewer vehicles than Baseline ($2619.0$ versus $2698.1$) despite its small queue reduction. PPO Online WCE completes more vehicles ($2905.4$ versus $2763.5$), with $0.8$ versus $118.6$ pending vehicles and no teleports in either method. These results do not support uniform external-demand superiority or across-training-seed stability. The Monaco supplementary campaign remains unexecuted pending route and historical-provenance validation.
